# Revision 0.4.0 — the valuation question, reduced and then found in the literature

Revision 0.3.0 answered the digit question for the residue and sharpened what
was left: is `v_3` off the central line **3-regular** in the transducer sense,
as the carry count is on the central line by Kummer?

The answer is **no in general**, the criterion is sharp, and it is not ours.
Two steps.

## Step one: the amplitude is a polynomial in `N`

For fixed `(A, R, f)`,

```
alpha = (-1)^A Σ_{k=0}^{d} C(N,k) · C(A+d-k-1, d-k),    d = R - 1 - f,
```

and `N = A + R - delta` occurs only inside `C(N,k)`, a polynomial in `N` of
degree `k`. So `alpha` is an **integer-valued polynomial in `N` of degree
exactly `d`**, the leading term coming from `k = d` with coefficient `1/d!`.

That is elementary, and it should have been noticed two revisions ago. It
reframes the whole question: the dossier is asking about the 3-adic valuation
of the values of a fixed polynomial, which is a far more standard object than
"the valuation of an amplitude off the central line" made it sound.

## Step two: that question has a published answer

> Zhang Shu and Jia-Yan Yao, *Analytic functions over `Z_p` and `p`-regular
> sequences*, C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 349 (2011) 947–952.
> <https://www.numdam.org/item/CRMATH_2011__349_17-18_947_0/>
>
> Bartosz Sobolewski, *On `k`-regularity of sequences of valuations and last
> nonzero digits*, Ramanujan J. 61 (2023) 1151–1195, Theorem 2.3 and
> Remark 2.2. <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.09922>

For an analytic `f` on `Z_p` with root set `R_f` **inside `Z_p`**, the sequence
`v_p(f(n))` is

| | |
|---|---|
| `R_f = ∅` | **periodic**, with a power of `p` as period |
| `∅ ≠ R_f ⊂ Q` | **`p`-regular** |
| `R_f ⊄ Q` | **not regular** |

Sobolewski's form allows roots in `N`, which matters here because amplitude
polynomials do vanish at integers.

So for this dossier: **`v_3` is 3-regular for a family exactly when every root
of that family's polynomial lying in `Z_3` is rational.** Families that acquire
an irrational 3-adic root — which is what happens as soon as the degree grows —
are not regular. The question posed in 0.3.0 is answered, in the negative, with
a sharp criterion, and the credit belongs to Shu, Yao and Sobolewski.

## What was checked here

The theorem is cited, not reproved. What is checked is that it applies, and
that the reduction to it is right.

| check | result |
|---|---|
| the amplitude really is a polynomial in `N`, by interpolation tested far outside the interpolation range | 10 families, all |
| families classified by their root set in `Z_3`, exactly | 10 |
| measured 3-kernel rank behaves as the trichotomy says | **10 / 10** |
| controls on plain polynomials of known root structure | **7 / 7** |
| every branch of the trichotomy exercised by a control | yes |
| both rank behaviours present among the families | yes |

Measured ranks: degree-1 families stabilize at 2; `2,4,1` has no root in `Z_3`
at all and stabilizes; the degree-3-and-up families climb `1, 4, 7, 9, 11, 13,
15` — two more per level, with no sign of stopping.

A truncated window can only **lower** an observed rank, so climbing is
evidence that the module is genuinely larger, while stabilizing is not proof
that it stops. The controls are what make the measurement readable in both
directions, which is why there is one per branch rather than a sample of
convenient cases.

## Two defects caught, both in the classifier rather than the mathematics

**Labelling roots in the wrong field.** The first control set labelled
`x² + 3x − 3` as having an irrational root — true over `C`, irrelevant here. It
has **no root in `Z_3`**, so the theorem puts it in the periodic branch, and
the rank duly stabilized while the control expected it to climb. The label was
wrong, not the test.

**Hensel survivors are not roots.** The first classifier tested for a root in
`Z_3` by lifting residues modulo `3^k` and asking whether any survived. That is
unreliable while rational roots are still present: `(x+2)(x−1)` has two
rational roots three apart, so the product is divisible by 9 at every lift and
survivors persist with no further root behind them. It was classified
"irrational root" and mispredicted. Dividing out every rational root first
makes the test correct — and then correct by compactness, since a residue
surviving to all depths in a polynomial with no rational roots is a genuine
irrational root of `Z_3`.

Both were caught by the controls rather than by inspection, which is the
argument for having one control per branch instead of per result.
