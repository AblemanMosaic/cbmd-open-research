# Revision 0.3.0 — the digit question, answered for the residue

Revision 0.2.0 recorded a negative and left a question: off the central line
`delta = 2A + R`, the valuation `v_3` is not a function of the low base-three
digits of `A` and `N = A + R - delta` together with `d`, at depth 2 or 3. The
standing obligation was to *settle whether a full-digit base-three criterion
exists off the central line*.

It does, for the residue, and the automaton is two states. The earlier negative
was about **windows**, not about digits — and about two things missing from a
window at once.

## The reduction

Dropping the 3-adic unit `2^(R-1)`,

```
alpha(A, R, delta, f) = (-1)^A [y^d] (1+y)^N (1-y)^(-A),
d = R - 1 - f,   N = A + R - delta.
```

Over `F_3` we have `(1 ± y)^(3^m) = 1 ± y^(3^m)`, so a series agrees up to
degree `d` with the one whose exponent is reduced modulo `3^m`, for any
`3^m > d`. Writing `M = N mod 3^m` and `B = (-A) mod 3^m`,

```
alpha ≡ (-1)^A [y^d] (1+y)^M (1-y)^B    (mod 3).
```

Two things are worth naming here.

First, `M` and `B` are the **3-adic** digit expansions of `N` and `-A`. When
the integer is negative those expansions are eventually all twos. A window on
the low digits of `A` and `N` as *integers* was therefore reading the wrong
object, and no depth would have saved it.

Second, the reduction is checked rather than assumed: the census runs the
automaton at two different moduli and requires the answers to agree, so the
exponent reduction is not an artifact of one choice of `m`.

## The automaton

Lucas factors both exponents: `(1+y)^M = ∏_i (1 + y^(3^i))^(M_i)`. Position `i`
contributes `a_i` copies of `3^i` from the first product and `b_i` from the
second, with `0 ≤ a_i ≤ M_i` and `0 ≤ b_i ≤ B_i`. So `a_i + b_i ≤ 4`, and
matching the base-three digits of `d` needs a **carry** in `{0, 1}`.

Reading the digit triples `(M_i, B_i, d_i)` least significant first, with that
carry as the entire state and the sign `(-1)^(b_i)` folded into the weight,
computes `alpha mod 3`. Two states, no other memory.

That is the second thing a window was missing. A fixed-depth window reads
digits in isolation; this criterion is digit-local only once information is
allowed to propagate upward.

## What this settles, and what it does not

**Settled.** A full-digit criterion exists off the central line for the
**residue** `alpha mod 3`, constructively, and the obstruction recorded in
0.2.0 is explained rather than merely contradicted: it ruled out windows
without carries over integer digits, and the criterion needs 3-adic digits
with carries.

For transport this is the operative predicate. A zero certificate carried
across a ring boundary depends on whether the class vanishes modulo three, and
that is now decidable from the digits.

**Not settled.** The valuation itself. `v_3` is unbounded — it reaches 10
inside the box measured here — so it is not an automatic function with a
finite output alphabet, for the trivial reason that such a function has
bounded range. Whether `v_3` is 3-regular in the transducer sense, as the
carry count is on the central line by Kummer, is left open and is now the
sharper form of the remaining question.

## Evidence

[`verification/digit_criterion.py`](../verification/digit_criterion.py) imports
only the dossier's own amplitude and the standard library.

| quantity | value |
|---|---|

| disagreements | **0** |
| zero mod 3 / nonzero mod 3 | 29,927 / 54,703 |
| same automaton with the carry deleted → wrong | **28,195** |
| modulus mismatches between `3^m` and `3^(m+2)` | **0** |
| closed form vs defining Newton construction | 240 checked, 0 disagreements |
| largest `v_3` observed | 10 |

The carry row is the one that matters. Without it the automaton is wrong on
roughly a third of the box, which is what makes "carries, not more digits" the
content of this revision rather than a presentational detail.

The last two rows are controls rather than results: the modulus check guards
the reduction, and the defining-construction check guards against the closed
form having drifted — the dossier's own discipline, since the closed form is
what the automaton is derived from.
