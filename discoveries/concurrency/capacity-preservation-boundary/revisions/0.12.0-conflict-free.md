# Revision 0.12.0 — zero conflict places polynomial, one coNP-complete

Revision 0.11.0 left *conflict-free* nets open: every place has at most one
consuming transition, with any number of producers. They are polynomial —
under the convention this dossier uses throughout, that **every transition has
at least one input place** — at arbitrary weights and capacities.

The free-choice nets of revision 0.10.0 have exactly **one** place with more
than one consumer (`g`). So the boundary is now sharp in a structural
parameter:

| places with several consumers | status |
|---|---|
| 0 (conflict-free) | **polynomial** (this revision; written) |
| 1 | **coNP-complete** (0.10.0; reduction compiled) |

This is a different axis from revision 0.8.0, where the number of multi-input
*transitions* gives fixed-parameter tractability. Conflict places give no such
gradation: one is already as hard as the general problem.

## The procedure (written argument)

Fix the transition `t0` that a bad marking ordinarily enables; its inputs get
the floor `lo(q) = pre(t0,q)`. Call a transition *obligated* when all its inputs
are high enough that only an overflow can block it. `t0` is obligated. An
obligated `t` picks an output `q` with `post > pre` whose overflow threshold
`b = max(cap − post + pre + 1, lo(q))` is at most `cap(q)`, and sets
`m(q) = b`. Then:

* the chain **ends** if `q` has no consumer, or its consumer is `t` or `t0`, or
  its consumer has two or more inputs, or the consumer's own lower literal
  still holds at `b`;
* otherwise the single-input consumer of `q` is now ordinarily enabled, so it is
  obligated, and the chain continues there.

A bad marking with this `t0` exists iff `t0` survives the greatest fixpoint

```
Alive = { t : some option of t ends, or leads into Alive }
```

**Completeness.** At a bad marking, every ordinarily enabled transition is
capacity-dead, so it overflows at some output; that output's threshold is met,
and if the chain does not end there, the single-input consumer is ordinarily
enabled too. The ordinarily enabled transitions therefore form a
post-fixpoint, and all of them are `Alive`.

**Soundness.** Fix one surviving option per `Alive` transition and follow it
from `t0`. Set the chosen places, and `t0`'s inputs, to their required values,
taking the maximum where a place is required twice, and set every other place
to `0`. Each chain transition overflows at its chosen place. A transition off
the chain has an input at `0` — *this is where every transition needing an
input place is used* — unless all its inputs were set high. That cannot happen:
`t0`'s inputs feed only `t0`; a chain place whose consumer continues the chain
feeds a chain transition; and the chain stops at the first multi-input consumer
it meets, so such a consumer has only one high input.

Cost: `T` fixpoint computations over a graph with `T` nodes and at most `PT`
edges, independent of the magnitude of weights and capacities.

## Census (`verification/conflict_free.py`, `evidence/conflict_free.json`)

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| random conflict-free nets, every transition with an input (≤ 5 places, ≤ 4 transitions, weights and capacities ≤ 3) | 20,000 |
| agreements with brute force over all admissible markings | **20,000** |
| preserving / not preserving | 13,538 / 6,462 |
| with a multi-input transition / of those not preserving | 7,368 / 2,530 |
| wrong verdicts: no `t0` floor | 5,120 |
| wrong verdicts: multi-input consumer treated as continuing | 148 |
| wrong verdicts: consumer's lower literal ignored | 610 |
| wrong verdicts on nets **with** input-less transitions (hypothesis test) | 1,061 of 5,000 |
| conflict places in every 0.10.0 free-choice net | exactly 1 |

A separate sanity pass on larger nets (4–7 places, 2–6 transitions) gave 3,000
agreements in 3,000, 1,661 of them non-preserving; it is not wired into the
bundled checks.

Every ablation breaks the procedure. So does dropping the hypothesis: on nets
with input-less transitions the procedure is wrong in about one case in five.
Such transitions are always ordinarily enabled, so there are several sources of
obligation, and their chains can meet at a multi-input transition and fill
it — the interaction the single-chain argument rules out. A naive extension
that checks each source independently is still wrong, in 3 of 14,087 nets
tried.

## Standing

| class | status |
|---|---|

| marked graphs | polynomial (0.11.0; written) |
