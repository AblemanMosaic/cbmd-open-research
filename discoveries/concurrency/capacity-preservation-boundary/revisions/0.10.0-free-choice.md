# Revision 0.10.0 — free choice does not help

Free-choice nets are the classical subclass in which many Petri-net problems
become tractable or acquire structure theory (Commoner's theorem, the rank
theorem, polynomial liveness for bounded free-choice systems). The natural
question after revision 0.4.0 is whether capacity preservation is one of them.

It is not. The hardness survives in **strict free-choice** nets — a place with
more than one output transition is the only input place of each of them — with
unit weights, **every capacity equal to 1**, and one or two input places per
transition.

## The construction

For a CNF on `n` variables: places `x_i`, `x̄_i`, `g`, all of capacity 1.

| transition | inputs | outputs | blocked exactly when |
|---|---|---|---|
| `E_i` | `x_i, x̄_i` | — | not both hold |
| `H_i` | `g` | `x_i, x̄_i` | `g` empty, or `x_i` or `x̄_i` holds |
| `C_k` | `g` | literal places of clause `k` | `g` empty, or a literal holds |

Only `g` has several output transitions, and every transition consuming from
`g` consumes from `g` alone: strict free choice.

**Why it works.** A bad marking must ordinarily enable some transition. An
`E_i` has no outputs, so if it is ordinarily enabled it is capacity-enabled —
impossible at a capacity-dead marking. So the enabled transition consumes from
`g`, which forces `g` to hold. Then each `E_i` blocked says *not both*, and
each `C_k` blocked says *some literal holds*: a satisfying assignment, read off
`x_i`. Conversely a satisfying assignment, with `g` full, is a bad marking,
`H_0` being the enabled transition.

Compared with revision 0.4.0 the probes and the zero-capacity place are gone,
and so is the guard's family of `(¬x_i ∨ g)` clauses: the free-choice shape
does their work.

`0 < n` is stated, not hidden: with no variables and no clauses the net has no
transitions and preserves vacuously, while the empty CNF is satisfiable.

## The `H` family is nearly idle — a prediction, tested

The compiled backward direction never uses `H`. The forward direction uses it
only to have *some* `g`-transition to enable, which a clause transition
provides whenever there is at least one clause. So the proof predicts: removing
`H` breaks the reduction **on the empty CNF and nowhere else**. The census
confirms it (gate `ablation_H_breaks_only_empty_cnf`). This is the same
phenomenon revision 0.9.0 found in the guard — a family the prose justifies
and the proof does not need.

Removing `E` instead must break it, since `E` is the only multi-input family
and without it the nets are single-input, hence polynomial (revision 0.6.0).

## Relation to known results

Cheng, Esparza and Palsberg (1995, Thm 14) prove **deadlock** NP-complete for
free-choice nets — whether some *reachable* marking is dead. That is a
different question: this one quantifies over admissible markings and involves
no reachability (see `review/PRIOR_ART.md`, revision 0.4.0). The two results
are parallel, not one a corollary of the other, and no novelty is claimed
beyond the specific statement.

| class | status |

| `k` multi-input transitions | FPT in `k` (0.8.0) |

Membership in coNP and the polynomial running time of the construction remain
written arguments, as before.
