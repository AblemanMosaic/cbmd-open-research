# Revision 0.11.0 — marked graphs are polynomial

Revision 0.10.0 showed the hardness survives in strict free-choice nets. This
revision locates the other side of that line among the classical subclasses:
in a **marked graph** — every place has at most one consuming and at most one
producing transition, self-loops allowed — preservation is decidable in
polynomial time, for **arbitrary weights and capacities**.

Multi-input transitions are allowed, so this is not the single-input case of
revision 0.6.0 in disguise: 6,878 of the 20,000 censused nets have one, and
2,396 of those fail to preserve.

## The argument (written, not compiled)

A transition `t` is capacity-blocked exactly when one of its literals holds:

```
m(q) ≤ pre(t,q) − 1                         for each input q of t        (le)
m(q) ≥ cap(q) − post(t,q) + pre(t,q) + 1    for each q with post > pre   (ge)
```

In a marked graph each place carries at most one `le` literal (from its
consumer) and at most one `ge` literal (from its producer), so every variable
occurs in at most two clauses. A bad marking is admissible, blocks every
transition, and ordinarily enables some `t0`. Fix `t0`; ordinary enabledness
raises the floor of each input `q` of `t0` to `pre(t0,q)`. Then, place by
place:

* a literal unsatisfiable in its domain is dropped;
* one surviving literal satisfies its clause outright;
* two jointly satisfiable literals, or a self-loop, satisfy both clauses
  outright — the place occurs nowhere else;
* two mutually exclusive literals form an **edge** between their clauses: any
  value satisfies at most one, and some value satisfies either.

So a bad marking with this `t0` exists iff the edges can be oriented to give
every unsatisfied clause an incoming edge, which holds iff every connected
component contains an already-satisfied clause or has at least as many edges
as vertices. One union-find pass per choice of `t0`: `O(T · (P + T))`
arithmetic operations, independent of the magnitude of capacities and weights.

This is the classical tractability of SAT with every variable occurring at most
twice, adapted to threshold literals; no novelty is claimed for the technique.

Both ablations break the procedure, so the floor and the orientation count are
each load-bearing.

## Where the line now sits

| class | places | status |
|---|---|---|
| single input place per transition | any | polynomial (0.6.0) |
| marked graphs | ≤ 1 consumer, ≤ 1 producer | **polynomial** (this revision; written) |
| `k` multi-input transitions | any | FPT in `k` (0.8.0) |
| strict free-choice, capacities 1 | shared places are sole inputs | coNP-complete (0.10.0) |
| general | any | coNP-complete (0.4.0) |

**Open, and named:** *conflict-free* nets — each place has at most one
consumer but any number of producers. Every variable then has one `le` literal
and arbitrarily many `ge` literals. Neither argument applies directly: the
free-choice reduction needs a place with several consumers to broadcast
"`g` holds", and the two-occurrence argument needs at most one producer. A
first look suggests that truth cannot fan out in this class (a place can
complete at most one transition's input set), which points toward
tractability, but nothing is claimed.
