# Revision 0.3.0 — the draining regime, characterized

Revision 0.2.0 proved that an **increasing** net — every transition strictly
increases the count at some place — preserves exactly when it cannot fire at
all, and said the hypothesis fails precisely for transitions that **drain**.
That was where the result stopped, and it was listed as open.

This closes it for the unit-weight flow family: each transition takes one token
from a place and either moves it to another place or discards it. Drains are
the whole point of the family, so the all-full witness of 0.2.0 does not apply
and a real characterization is needed.

## The condition

A set `S` of places is an **obstruction** when

1. every member has positive capacity;
2. no drain takes from a member — a drain is never blocked, so a member feeding
   one could always fire;
3. every mover out of a member lands in `S`, or in a place of capacity zero —
   those are the only two ways a mover can be blocked;
4. some mover takes from a member.

> **Theorem.** `PRESERVES(N, C)` holds if and only if no obstruction exists.

Condition 3's second branch is the part that is easy to miss: a mover aimed at
a zero-capacity place is blocked permanently without that place being in `S` at
all. Condition 4 is the part that was in fact missed — see below.

## The proof

*Obstruction ⇒ failure.* Set `m(q) = C(q)` on `S` and `0` elsewhere; this is
admissible. Any transition taking from outside `S` is not ordinarily enabled.
Any transition taking from inside `S` is not a drain, by 2, so it is a mover
with destination `d`; by 3 either `d ∈ S`, where `m(d) = C(d)` and one more
token overflows, or `C(d) = 0`, where `m(d) = 0` and one more token overflows.
So `m` is capacity-dead. By 4 some mover takes from `S`, and by 1 its source
holds at least one token, so `m` is ordinarily enabled. Preservation fails.

*Failure ⇒ obstruction.* Take a failing `m` and set `S = {q : m(q) ≥ 1}`.
Condition 1 is admissibility. A drain from `S` would be capacity-enabled, which
contradicts capacity-deadness, giving 2. For a mover from `S` to `d`,
capacity-deadness forces `m(d) + 1 > C(d)`, and with `m(d) ≤ C(d)` that gives
`m(d) = C(d)`; if `C(d) > 0` then `d ∈ S`, otherwise `C(d) = 0`, which is 3.
The transition that was ordinarily enabled cannot be a drain — a drain would be
capacity-enabled — so it is a mover taking from `S`, which is 4. ∎

Both directions compile; no finiteness is used.

## Relation to the earlier results

This is the filled-set obstruction of `CBMD-DYN-WEIGHTED-CAPACITY-001` again,
widened twice: once to let a transition discard its token rather than deliver
it, and once to let a zero-capacity receiver do the blocking instead of a full
one. The chip-firing live-set conditions are the special case with no drains
and no zero capacities, and the all-full obstruction of revision 0.2.0 is the
case where `S` can be taken to be every place.

The third row from the bottom group matters on its own. Lean proves an
existential statement — *no obstruction exists* — while the script decides it by
peeling places that cannot belong to any obstruction. Those are different
objects, and the agreement is checked by brute force over all subsets of places
rather than assumed. The ablations are the anti-vacuity control: a condition
whose removal changes nothing was never part of the characterization.

Dropping a condition only ever produces incompleteness, never unsoundness,
which is the expected direction: a weaker obstruction test finds obstructions
more often, so it predicts failure more often.

## A defect caught by the gates

The first draft of the condition had only clauses 1–3. It was sound but
incomplete, and the smallest witness is three movers `0 → 1` with capacities
`(0, 0, 1)`: place 2 has capacity 1 and no outgoing mover, so `{2}` passes
clauses 1–3, but nothing takes from place 2, so filling it blocks nothing and
the net does preserve. Clause 4 is exactly the repair. The exactness gate failed
with 390 incomplete cases in the smallest box rather than passing quietly, and
the ablation row for condition 4 is now kept as a permanent control.
