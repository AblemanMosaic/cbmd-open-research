# Revision 0.9.0 — the hardness reduction, compiled

Revision 0.4.0 proved `PRESERVES` coNP-complete at two input places by a
written argument and checked the reduction only by census: 240 random
instances plus every CNF in a small box, zero disagreements. This revision
compiles the reduction's correctness.

## What is compiled

`Reduction.lean` — the net built from a CNF `F` (atoms at capacity 1, one
zero-capacity place `z`, one transition per clause, one probe per atom):

| declaration | content |
|---|---|
| `clause_capEnabled_iff` | on admissible markings, a clause transition is capacity-enabled ⟺ the read-off assignment falsifies the clause |
| `preserves_iff_no_nonzero_model` | `Preserves (net F) cap ↔ ¬ NonzeroModel F`, under `NoTautology F` |
| `probe_input_places`, `clause_input_places` | probes have exactly one input place; a clause transition's input places are its negative literals |

`Guard.lean` — the guarded encoding of an arbitrary CNF `G` on `n` atoms:

| declaration | content |
|---|---|
| `guard_noTautology` | the guarded CNF satisfies the hypothesis above |
| `satisfiable_iff_guard_nonzero` | `G` satisfiable ⟺ its guarded form has a model that is not all-false |
| `guard_neg_count` | every guarded clause has at least one and at most two negative literals |
| **`preserves_iff_unsatisfiable`** | **`Preserves (net (guard G)) cap ↔ ¬ Satisfiable G`** |

All depend on `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]` at most
(`guard_noTautology` on `[propext, Quot.sound]`). Mathlib-free, no `sorry`.

So every transition of the constructed net has one or two input places
(`guard_neg_count` with `clause_input_places`, and `probe_input_places`), and
the net preserves exactly when the input is unsatisfiable. That is the whole
mathematical content of the hardness direction.

## A hypothesis the census never stated

`NoTautology`: no clause contains an atom with both signs. A clause `x ∨ ¬x`
becomes a transition that consumes and produces at `x`; it is capacity-enabled
whenever `x` holds a token, although the clause is satisfied. So
`preserves_iff_no_nonzero_model` is **false** without it. The guarded encoding
never produces such a clause — it sends `x` and `¬x` to different atoms — and
`guard_noTautology` proves that, so the end-to-end theorem needs no hypothesis.
But a reader reusing the clause-level construction on raw CNFs would have hit
it, and the written argument of 0.4.0 does not mention it.

## A finding the formalization exposed

The written argument justifies the guard clauses family by family. The
compiled backward direction (`nonzero_to_satisfiable`) never uses family 2,
`x_i ∨ x̄_i ∨ ¬g`. A non-all-false model already forces `g` (families 3 and 4),
and families 1 and 5 then force a satisfying assignment of `G` by reading
`x_i` alone. Family 2 is needed only in the forward direction's *construction*
— and even there the lifted assignment satisfies it automatically. It makes
each pair one-hot, which the prose relies on for readability; the proof does
not. Nothing is withdrawn: the reduction stands with family 2, and is not
claimed to stand without it, since that variant was not separately checked.

## The bridge

A compiled theorem about a different net than the census tested would certify
nothing about the census. `verification/reduction_bridge.py` transcribes the
Lean definitions literally and compares them, formula by formula, with
`hardness.build(*hardness.guarded_cnf(...))`:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| formulas (exhaustive box + random pass) | 404 |
| nets differing (capacities, or transitions as a multiset) | **0** |
| `NoTautology` failures | **0** |
| mismatches under a deliberately wrong transcription | 375 |

The last row is the negative control: a transcription that keeps `G`'s
negative literals on `x_a` instead of moving them to `x̄_a` must be detected,
or the comparison would be idle. Transition order differs (Lean groups guard
clauses by family, `hardness.py` interleaves them per variable); `Preserves`
quantifies over all transitions, so order is immaterial.
