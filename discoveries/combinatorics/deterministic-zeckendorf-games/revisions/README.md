| 0.5.4 | Sharpened the recorded deviation observation and corrected its reading. `L_CS(n) − c·n` was recorded as growing "like `−0.42 log_φ n`", which reads as universal; it is not. `D(n)/log_φ n` depends on the Zeckendorf shape of `n`, at index 1000 running from `−0.120` (`F_m + F_{⌊m/2⌋}`) through `−0.176` (`F_m`) and `−0.392` (maximal density) to `−0.675` (`F_m − 1`), a factor of five, and it is not monotone in the density. The `−0.42` is the value for typical `n`, and random sampling concentrates there — for `n` in `[10⁸⁰, 10⁸¹)` the ratio stayed in `[−0.481, −0.369]` — which is why it looked universal. Noted that the odd- and even-index alternating families differ sharply (`−0.675` against `−0.392`), the natural starting point for an exact account given that `c` is built from odd-index Fibonacci numbers. Also searched for the counterexample that would refute `Θ(log n)` pointwise, namely a family with bounded `D`: none found, the two-term minimum of `|D|` sitting at `k/m ≈ 1/3` and growing with `m` (ratios `0.0765, 0.0712, 0.0602, 0.0587, 0.0626` at `m = 100..600`). New required gate `CS_DEVIATION_SHAPES` (`cs_deviation_shapes.py`, about 2 s) asserts that the spread exceeds a factor 2, that the two-term minimum grows in `m`, and that the deviation never vanishes. |
