# Revision 0.3.0 — a characterization over arbitrary digraphs

Versions 0.1.0 and 0.2.0 gave sufficiency on forward-ordered graphs and
impossibility on cyclic ones. Neither covered arbitrary digraphs with arbitrary
capacities, where a vertex may be unable to fire at all.

## The live set

Write `Live v` for `outdegree v <= capacity v`: the vertices that can ever hold
their own firing threshold inside the capacity box. A vertex outside it is
**permanently ordinarily stable**, since its chips never exceed its capacity.
That single observation is what lets the argument run on arbitrary graphs.

## The characterization

Transfer holds for every admissible initial state exactly when:

1. **ordered on live** — the arcs among live vertices admit a rank with
   `rank u < rank v` whenever `mult u v > 0` (that is, the live subgraph is acyclic);
2. **bounded on live** — `outdegree v + mult u v <= capacity v + 1` for live `u -> v`;
3. **live-closed** — a live vertex sends only to live vertices.

Condition 3 is new and was not anticipated. A live vertex pointing at a dead one
can be blocked forever: the dead receiver never fires, so once it fills, the
sender is stuck, while ordinary firing proceeds because it has no capacity at all.

## Exactness and minimality

Over all digraphs with `n <= 3`, multiplicities `<= 2`, unit sink arcs and every
capacity vector in `0..d(v)+2` — **798,025 systems** — the predicate agrees with
the exact transfer property in every case: 119,251 both true, 678,774 both
false, **zero** unsound and **zero** incomplete.

Each condition is separately necessary. Dropping one and re-running the census:

| dropped | systems where the weakened predicate is wrong |
|---|---|
| ordered on live | 63,847 |
| bounded on live | 4,998 |
| live-closed | 393,192 |

No dropped condition produces an incomplete predicate, so the three are
independent and minimal, not merely jointly sufficient.

## Both obstructions are one obstruction

`filled_set_obstruction` proves a single statement: take any set whose **live**
members each have a positive arc back into the set, fill the set to capacity,
empty everything else. Live members are blocked by full successors; dead members
sit at capacity, which is below their threshold; non-members are empty. The
state is capacity-terminal and not ordinarily stable.

The cycle case and the live-to-dead case are both instances. In the first the
successor is the next cycle vertex; in the second the live vertex points at the
dead one, and the dead member needs no successor at all because it is never
live. `liveToDeadFilledSet` builds the two-member instance explicitly.

Because the construction fills vertices *to their own capacities*, it is immune
to capacity enlargement in both cases.

## Standing

`live_terminal_implies_ordinary_stable` and `filled_set_obstruction` compile
with no proof escapes; `dead_is_ordinarily_stable` and `liveToDeadFilledSet`
depend on no axioms at all. The characterization's **exactness** is established
by exhaustive census over the stated box, not by proof: sufficiency is proved,
and necessity of each condition is witnessed by the obstruction plus the
ablation, but no proof is offered that the three conditions are exhaustive over
all digraphs. That remains open.
